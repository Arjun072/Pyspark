The Last Signal — Extended Ending

Ravi worked the night shift at the old coastal radio station, a place most ships no longer used. Satellites had taken over years ago, but the government kept the station running “just in case.”

Every night was the same — waves crashing, machines humming, and the endless blink of silent frequencies.

Until one stormy midnight.

A signal cut through the static.

Weak. Broken. Repeating.

“…if anyone hears this… we are still here…”

Ravi froze. The timestamp on the transmission showed a date from **27 years ago**.

He adjusted the dials, heart racing. The voice came again — clearer this time.

“…power failing… island grid collapsing… please respond…”

Ravi checked the archived maps. The island mentioned had been abandoned after a mysterious evacuation decades earlier.

Yet the signal was live.

Hands shaking, he pressed the transmit key.

“This is Coastal Station 14. I read you. Identify.”

Static roared.

Then — a whisper.

“…you shouldn’t have answered…”

All the equipment died at once.

Lights out. Frequencies silent.

Only one thing remained active — the recording console.

On the screen, a new file appeared automatically:

**incoming_log_27years_ago.wav**

---

The backup generator kicked in after a minute, flooding the room with dim yellow light.

Ravi stared at the file.

No system in the station could create audio logs on its own.

Still, he played it.

At first — only ocean wind.

Then voices.

Hundreds of them.

Layered. Overlapping. Desperate.

“…don’t let them hear you…”
“…they track the signal…”
“…we thought rescue came…”

Ravi’s breath shortened.

Then he heard something that turned his blood cold.

A voice in the recording — clear, recent.

His own.

“This is Coastal Station 14. I read you. Identify.”

He slammed the stop button.

The timestamp on the audio file began changing in real time.

Rewinding.

Years dropping backward.

2026… 2020… 2005… 1999…

Finally stopping at:

**1999 — The Year The Island Was Evacuated**

The console speaker crackled again — but now the sound wasn’t coming from the recording.

It was coming through the live receiver.

“…Coastal Station 14… we hear you… transmission link established…”

Ravi stepped back slowly.

On the dark radar screen, a single green dot appeared offshore.

Another beside it.

Then dozens.

Ships.

All broadcasting on the same dead frequency.

The final message came through — calm, relieved:

“…thank you for reopening the channel… we’ve been waiting 27 years to come back…”

Outside, beyond the storm, faint lights began rising from the sea — moving toward the shore.

Toward the station.

Toward him.

The receiver locked on by itself.

And the transmit light turned red again.
